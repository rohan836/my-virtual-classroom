Slowing the rush of electric current

Electricity wants to race through a wire as fast as it can. A resistor adds a thin carbon path that slows the current down, protecting delicate parts like LEDs from burning out.

law
V = I × R (Ohm's Law). Raising resistance cuts the current; raising voltage pushes more current through.
try
Open the cutaway view, change the color bands, or switch to the LED circuit and drop resistance to 10 Ω!
notice
Higher resistance carves more spiral turns into the carbon film, making the path longer and harder for electrons to cross.
in life
Inside every phone charger, TV, LED lamp, and volume knob — billions are made every single day.
found by
Georg Simon Ohm discovered the voltage-current-resistance rule in 1827.
